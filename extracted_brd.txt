BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT
The Bling Haven - International Jewellery & Ornaments E-Commerce Platform
Enterprise Web Storefront • Customer Account • Admin Portal • Secure Commerce API
Prepared for: International Client
Document version: 1.0 | Date: 15 August 2026 | Status: Baseline / Development-Ready
1. Executive Summary
This BRD defines the business, functional, UI/UX, technical, security, operational and non-functional requirements for a premium international e-commerce platform specializing in jewellery, ornaments and related luxury products. The platform is designed for global customers and must support multi-currency commerce, international shipping, localized content, secure payments, high traffic, fraud resistance, strong privacy controls and an administrator portal.
The solution should be treated as a security-sensitive commerce platform. It should not promise to be 'unhackable'; the objective is defense-in-depth: minimize attack surface, prevent common and advanced attacks, detect abnormal activity, contain incidents, recover quickly and maintain auditable evidence.
2. Product Vision & Objectives
Deliver a premium, fast, mobile-first jewellery shopping experience for international customers.
Provide high-quality product discovery through categories, filters, search, collections and editorial content.
Support secure product purchase, payment, order tracking, returns/refunds and customer self-service.
Provide a controlled admin portal for catalog, inventory, orders, customers, promotions, content and reporting.
Protect customer PII, account credentials, payment flows, order data and business data using defense-in-depth controls.
Absorb legitimate traffic spikes and resist bot abuse, credential stuffing, scraping, inventory hoarding and DDoS attempts.
Support future expansion into multiple countries, currencies, languages, tax regimes and logistics providers.
3. Scope
3.1 In Scope
Public storefront and responsive customer experience.
Customer registration, login, password reset, MFA/step-up authentication and account management.
Product catalog, categories, collections, variants, materials, gemstones, dimensions, certification metadata and media.
Search, filtering, sorting, recommendations, recently viewed and wishlist.
Cart, coupon/promotion engine, shipping calculation, tax calculation and checkout.
Payment provider integration using hosted/tokenized payment components; no unnecessary storage of raw card data.
Order creation, payment status, fulfillment status, shipment tracking, cancellation, return/refund workflows.
International currency, locale, country, address and shipping support.
Admin portal with role-based access control and audit logging.
Inventory and stock reservation with concurrency controls.
CMS-like content for banners, collections, policies, FAQs and editorial pages.
Security controls: CDN/WAF, DDoS mitigation, rate limiting, bot management, fraud controls, secrets management, SIEM/logging.
Monitoring, backups, disaster recovery, incident response and security testing.
3.2 Out of Scope for Phase 1
Native mobile applications unless separately approved.
Marketplace / multi-vendor seller onboarding.
Cryptocurrency payments unless legally and operationally approved.
AI-generated product descriptions published automatically without human review.
Physical retail POS integration unless separately specified.
Storing full payment-card numbers or CVV in the application database.

6. Customer Journey
Customer lands on localized storefront through CDN/edge layer.
Customer browses collection/category or searches for a product.
Customer opens product detail page and reviews images, specifications, material, size, availability, shipping and returns.
Customer adds a variant to wishlist or cart.
Cart validates price, stock, promotions and country-specific constraints.
Checkout collects shipping/billing information and creates an expiring checkout session.
Payment is completed through a PCI-aligned payment provider flow.
Order service confirms payment and atomically reserves/commits stock.
Customer receives confirmation and can track order status.
Fulfillment and carrier events update shipment status through verified webhooks.
Customer can initiate eligible cancellation/return/refund according to policy.

13. UI/UX Requirements
Visual direction: premium luxury aesthetic with restrained typography, high-quality product photography, generous whitespace and clear conversion paths.
Desktop: mega navigation, search, account, wishlist and cart in persistent header; editorial hero and collection-led discovery.
Mobile: compact header, sticky cart/checkout actions, touch-friendly filters and image-first product browsing.
Product page: gallery left / information right on desktop; stacked gallery and purchase module on mobile.
Checkout: distraction-minimized, progress indicator, address autocomplete where lawful, shipping options, taxes and final total before payment.
Admin portal: data-dense but readable tables, filters, bulk actions, side drawers/modals, audit context and permission-aware controls.
Accessibility target: WCAG 2.2 AA as the design baseline, subject to final audit and applicable local requirements.
Every destructive action must require confirmation and show scope; high-risk actions may require re-authentication.

19. DDoS, Traffic Flooding & Bot Protection Plan
Place the storefront and public API behind a reputable CDN/WAF/DDoS edge service.
Do not expose the origin server directly to the public Internet; permit origin traffic only from approved edge addresses or private connectivity.
Apply WAF managed rules plus custom rules for authentication, checkout, search, API and admin routes.
Use endpoint-specific rate limits rather than a single global limit. Login, registration, password reset, search, cart, coupon, checkout and webhooks require different policies.
Use bot detection/challenges selectively. Avoid CAPTCHAs on every page; trigger friction when risk signals justify it.
Cache public catalog content aggressively at the edge. Never cache personalized account/order/payment responses.
Use request body size limits, header limits, query complexity limits, pagination caps and upstream timeouts.
Implement circuit breakers, queue backpressure and graceful degradation so non-critical features do not take down checkout.
Keep origin IPs private and rotate them after targeted exposure where necessary.
Maintain a documented DDoS runbook with escalation contacts, traffic baselines and emergency WAF rules.
Cloudflare's current guidance specifically recommends WAF custom rules, rate limiting and keeping the origin inaccessible from the public Internet except through the edge. Rate limiting can also target credential stuffing, scraping, programmatic buying and API resource exhaustion. citeturn1search4turn1search15
20. Payment Security & PCI DSS
Use a PCI-compliant payment service provider and hosted/tokenized payment components to reduce the application's card-data scope.
Do not store CVV or raw PAN in application databases, logs, analytics or support systems.
Verify payment status server-to-server or through authenticated webhooks; never accept a client-side 'payment successful' flag as proof.
Use idempotency keys for payment creation, capture and refund operations.
Validate amount, currency, order ID and merchant reference server-side before confirming an order.
Restrict and monitor administrative refund capabilities.
Maintain payment reconciliation between application orders and PSP settlement/reporting.
Use PCI DSS v4.0.1 as the baseline where the organization falls within PCI DSS scope. PCI SSC describes PCI DSS as a global baseline of technical and operational requirements for protecting payment account data. citeturn1search0turn1search7
21. Privacy & International Data Protection
Determine the legal jurisdictions in which customers are located and where the business operates before launch.
Use data minimization, purpose limitation, retention controls and transparent privacy notices.
Provide mechanisms for applicable access, correction, deletion and portability requests.
Record marketing consent separately from account creation and transactional communications.
Maintain cookie/analytics consent where required; do not assume consent merely because a user visits the site.
Control cross-border transfers and processor/subprocessor relationships. For EU personal data, GDPR principles include lawfulness/transparency, purpose limitation, data minimization, storage limitation, accuracy, integrity/confidentiality and accountability. citeturn1search1
When EU personal data is transferred outside the EU, use an applicable adequacy decision or safeguards such as Standard Contractual Clauses where required. citeturn1search23
22. Authentication, Authorization & Session Security
Use Argon2id or another modern password hashing scheme with appropriate work factors; never reversible encryption for passwords.
Use short-lived access tokens and secure refresh/session rotation strategy; revoke sessions after high-risk changes.
Admin accounts require MFA. Prefer phishing-resistant authentication for privileged users where supported.
Use RBAC with least privilege; consider attribute-based checks for warehouse, geography or business unit scopes.
Perform object-level authorization on every customer/order/address/resource lookup.
Use secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookies where cookie sessions are used; enforce HTTPS and HSTS.
Re-authenticate for password changes, payment method changes, high-value orders, refunds and admin security changes.
Provide session listing and revocation for customers and administrators.

24. Logging, Monitoring & Incident Response
Centralize application, WAF, API gateway, database audit, authentication, payment and infrastructure logs.
Use structured JSON logs with request/correlation ID, timestamp, actor, route, status, latency and security outcome.
Never log passwords, full payment-card data, authentication tokens or sensitive secrets.
Send security events to SIEM or equivalent centralized monitoring.
Create alerts for admin login anomalies, repeated authorization failures, brute force, abnormal checkout velocity, large exports, refund spikes, WAF blocks and payment webhook failures.
Maintain incident runbooks for DDoS, account takeover, payment fraud, data breach, credential leakage and supply-chain compromise.
Preserve evidence with synchronized time, restricted access and tamper-evident storage.

26. Performance & Scalability
CDN-cache public product and content pages; use revalidation for catalog updates.
Use PostgreSQL indexes for SKU, slug, category, status, price and search-supporting fields.
Use a search engine when catalog size or query complexity requires it; avoid unbounded SQL wildcard searches.
Use Redis for hot catalog data, session/checkout state where appropriate, rate-limit counters and job coordination.
Use asynchronous queues for email, image processing, search indexing, analytics and non-blocking integrations.
Use autoscaling at the application layer and load testing at expected peak plus safety margin.
Set database connection pool limits and upstream timeouts to avoid cascading failures.
Protect expensive operations such as search, recommendations and reports with caching, quotas and background processing.

30. Recommended Deployment Architecture
DNS → CDN/DDoS/WAF → load balancer/API gateway → application runtime → private PostgreSQL/Redis/object storage/integration network.
Separate production, staging and development environments with distinct credentials and data.
Admin portal should use a dedicated hostname and stricter WAF/rate-limit policies.
Database and Redis must not be directly reachable from the public Internet.
Use private subnets/security groups/firewalls and explicit egress controls.
Use managed secret storage and KMS-backed encryption where available.
CI/CD should build, test, scan, sign and deploy immutable artifacts.
Use infrastructure-as-code with peer review and drift detection.

35. API Error & Resilience Standard
Return stable machine-readable error codes and human-safe messages.
Never reveal stack traces, SQL errors, internal hostnames or secrets to clients.
Use 400-series responses for client errors, 401/403 for authentication/authorization, 409 for state conflicts and 429 for throttling.
Use retry-after headers for rate limiting where appropriate.
External integrations must use bounded retries with exponential backoff and jitter.
Payment and order operations must use idempotency and state machines to prevent duplicate financial actions.

42. Architecture Summary
The architecture intentionally separates customer experience, edge/security controls, backend domain services and private data/integration layers. The most important security principle is that the database, cache and internal services are never directly exposed to the Internet.

44. Final Recommendation
Build the first production version as a secure modular commerce platform rather than an overly fragmented microservice estate. Put the public surface behind CDN/WAF/DDoS controls, keep the origin private, enforce authorization at the service/resource level, use a PCI-compliant hosted/tokenized payment architecture, and treat observability, backup recovery and security testing as release requirements—not post-launch enhancements. This approach provides a practical path to international scale while keeping operational complexity manageable.

51. API & Integration Standards
Use versioned APIs such as /api/v1 and consistent resource naming.
Validate every request server-side with typed schemas, length limits, maximum body sizes and allowlisted fields.
Require bounded pagination and maximum page sizes on list APIs.
Check both function-level and object-level authorization on every protected resource.
Use idempotency keys for payment, final order creation, refund and other non-repeatable actions.
External calls must have timeouts, bounded retries with exponential backoff/jitter and circuit-breaker behavior.
Webhook endpoints must verify provider signatures, timestamps/replay controls and unique event IDs.
Do not expose stack traces, SQL errors, internal hostnames or sensitive identifiers to clients.

55. Global Traffic, DDoS & Server Protection
Place the public storefront and API behind an enterprise CDN/WAF/DDoS protection layer.
Keep the application origin private; allow inbound origin traffic only from approved edge networks or private connectivity.
Use different rate limits for login, password reset, search, cart, coupon, checkout, payment and webhooks.
Cache public catalog content aggressively; never cache personalized account/order/payment responses.
Apply request body, header, URL and query complexity limits.
Use bot management and risk-based challenges only where needed to avoid harming legitimate customer conversion.
Use asynchronous workers for email, image processing, indexing, analytics and reconciliation.
Use circuit breakers and graceful degradation for non-critical dependencies.
Monitor regional traffic and latency and maintain a DDoS incident runbook with emergency WAF rules and escalation contacts.

57. Commerce & Financial Integrity
Never trust client totals, prices, taxes, discounts or stock claims.

Separate payment authorization, capture, refund and reconciliation states.
Use provider-reference uniqueness plus idempotency to prevent duplicate capture/refund.

Run scheduled reconciliation between application payment states and PSP records.
Flag unusual payment failure, refund, country/payment mismatch and high-value order patterns for manual review.

Separate mandatory transactional communications from optional marketing preferences.
Restrict customer-support visibility of PII by role and audit access to sensitive records.
